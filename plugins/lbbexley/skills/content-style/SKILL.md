# Content style

## Why plain English

From the council's accessible language guide (B:HIVE, with Shaw Trust):

- the average reading age in the UK is about 9
- 1.7 million adults in England have literacy levels below those expected of an 11-year-old

Plain English isn't dumbing down. Specialists prefer it too, because it's quicker to read. Write public content for a reading age of about 9. Staff tools can use the words staff use every day, but still write plainly.

## The council's rules

1. **Have something to say.** If a sentence sounds meaningful but tells the reader nothing, delete it.
2. **Be specific.** "Collected every other Tuesday", not "collected regularly".
3. **Choose simple words.** Use, not utilise. Near, not in close proximity to. Help, not facilitate. Start, not commence. Use a longer word only when no other word will do.
4. **Write short sentences.** One thought per sentence. Aim for 15 to 20 words and never go over 25.
5. **Use the active voice.** "We'll email you", not "You will be emailed".
6. **Keep paragraphs short.** Two or three sentences.
7. **Cut fluff.** Words like "very", "little" and "rather" add nothing.
8. **Edit ruthlessly.** Shorten, delete and rewrite anything that doesn't add meaning.

## House style (harness defaults, based on GOV.UK)

- Talk to the reader as "you". The council is "we". Name it in full as "London Borough of Bexley" in headers, page titles and footers.
- Put the most important information first: what the reader needs to do, and by when.
- Sentence case for headings, buttons, labels and page titles. No capitals for emphasis, and no italics.
- Use bold sparingly, for a word or two that must not be missed.
- British English: organisation, colour, licence (noun), programme.
- Numerals for numbers ("3 days"). Dates as "1 April 2027". Times as "9am", "5:30pm", "midday". Use "to" in ranges: "9am to 5pm", "Monday to Friday".
- Money: "£25", "£1,250.50". Telephone numbers in the standard UK groups, like "020 7946 0000".
- No "e.g.", "i.e." or "etc.": write "for example", "that is", or finish the list.
- Write out an acronym the first time you use it, unless most people know it in its short form (NHS, MOT).
- No ampersands, except in names that use one. No exclamation marks.
- Link text says where the link goes. Never "click here" or "read more".
- Lists: introduce them with a lead-in line, start each item in lower case, and don't end items with full stops.

## No filler

AI tools and rushed writing produce the same padding. Remove it on sight:

- welcome lines and preamble: "Welcome to the…", "This page allows you to…", "Please find below…"
- vague promises: "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "empower", "unlock", "elevate", "leverage"
- emoji and decorative symbols (the standards check rejects emoji)
- placeholder text such as "lorem ipsum" (also rejected)
- summaries of what the reader has just read

The full list of words to avoid, with alternatives, and patterns for buttons, hints, errors, confirmations and emails: [words-and-patterns.md](words-and-patterns.md).

## Check it

- Paste public text into Read-Able (the council's recommended tool) and aim for a reading age of 9 to 11.
- Read it aloud. If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.
- Test with real users: if they ask what something means, rewrite it.
