# Third-party content

## The council is the publisher

Running a site that lets others post material is a continuing act of publication by the council, so it must have a system for moderating and removing unacceptable material (Code para 21). That includes:

- comments, forums, reviews and ratings on Power Pages sites
- free-text answers that are displayed back to other users
- partner or supplier content embedded in council pages or apps
- content a Copilot Studio agent pulls from websites through generative answers

## Rules

1. **Moderate before it's public.** Prefer pre-moderation for anything residents post. If posts appear straight away, there must be a way to report them and a named team that removes them quickly.
2. **Links out are generally fine** (para 22), even when the council doesn't control the other site. Take extra care before elections and be ready to remove them.
3. **Logos.** Third-party content can show the logos of organisations its authors are linked to, including political parties (para 23), but the council's own content must not promote a party.
4. **Partners follow the Code.** If the council helps a third party publish (funding, hosting or promotion), the Code's principles apply to that content too (para 25).
5. **Accessibility.** Third-party content the council pays for or controls must be accessible. Content the council neither funds nor controls can be exempt from the accessibility regulations; say so in the accessibility statement.
6. **Data protection.** If posts can include personal data, the DPIA covers it, and the privacy notice says how long posts are kept.

## Generative answers in agents

A public agent that answers from web content can repeat anything on the sites it searches. Limit knowledge sources to council-controlled sites (bexley.gov.uk and approved partner pages), and test what it says about politics, councillors and contested local issues. See the copilot-studio skill.
