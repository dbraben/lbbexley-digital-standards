# Publicity

## Why this applies to digital builds

The Local Government Act 1986 defines publicity as "any communication in whatever form, addressed to the public at large or a section of the public" (section 6). That covers public Power Pages sites, public Copilot Studio agents, emails to groups of residents, social posts, and any app that residents or partners use. The council must have regard to the Code in every decision on publicity (section 4), and the Code applies to websites, including material created by third parties that the council hosts (Code para 2).

Staff-only tools are mostly internal, but treat anything outsiders can see as publicity. If in doubt, apply this skill.

## The legal line that never moves

The council must not publish anything that, in whole or in part, appears to be designed to affect public support for a political party (section 2 of the Act). Nothing in the Code overrides this (Code para 3).

## The seven principles (Code para 4)

| Principle | What it means for a build |
|---|---|
| Lawful | Follows all relevant law. Paid-for advertising follows the ASA's Advertising Codes (para 5). Special rules before referendums (paras 7 to 9). |
| Cost effective | Value for money has been considered (para 10). Don't duplicate central government publicity unless local context adds value (para 12). |
| Objective | Facts and explanation, not persuasion. No campaign whose main purpose is to persuade people to hold a particular view on policy (para 16). Content about government policy is balanced and factually accurate (para 15). Paid-for advertising is clearly labelled as advertising (para 17). |
| Even-handed | Where an issue is politically controversial, present the different positions fairly (para 19). Content can show the work and views of individual councillors, except in a pre-election period, but must say when those views aren't the council's (para 20). |
| Appropriate | No newsletters that imitate commercial newspapers, and none more often than quarterly (para 28). Freely available in accessible formats (para 29). Clearly and unambiguously identified as coming from the council (para 30). |
| Equality and diversity | Publicity may try to change attitudes or behaviour on health, safety, crime prevention, race relations, equality, diversity and community issues (para 31), and should consider how it can support the equality duties (para 32). |
| Care in sensitive periods | Extra care before elections and referendums (paras 33 to 35). See [pre-election.md](pre-election.md). |

## In practice

- **Identity.** Every public page, app, agent and email shows clearly that it comes from the London Borough of Bexley, with the approved logo (brand skill).
- **Facts, not spin.** Describe services and policies plainly. Don't write persuasive copy about council decisions, and don't comment on national politics.
- **Councillors.** Councillor names, photos, quotes and logos can appear in normal times, as long as the content isn't designed to boost support for that person (para 24). Keep them out of anything built or published during a pre-election period, apart from factual election information.
- **Chatbots and generative answers.** A public agent speaks for the council. It must not give opinions on political questions, comment on parties or councillors, or say how to vote. Point election questions to Electoral Services' web pages. See the copilot-studio skill.
- **Third-party content.** Hosting comments, forums, partner content or external links makes the council the publisher. Moderation is required. See [third-party-content.md](third-party-content.md).
- **Accessible formats.** Publicity must be available in formats people can use (para 29), which the accessibility skill covers.
